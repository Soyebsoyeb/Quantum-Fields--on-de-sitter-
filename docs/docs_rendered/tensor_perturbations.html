Tensor perturbations and the boundary with quantum gravity

Status: tensor equation = symbolic verification; action normalisation and spectrum = quoted standard result + numerical check; everything on quantisation of gravity = conceptual discussion.

Decomposition

gμν​=gˉ​μν​+hμν​, gˉ​ = de Sitter. Gauge freedom hμν​→hμν​+∇ˉμ​ξν​+∇ˉν​ξμ​ (linearised diffeomorphisms). On the flat chart, decompose by SO(3) helicity: scalars (2 metric functions in a gauge-invariant combination plus constraints), vectors (decaying, no propagating dof in pure gravity), tensors hijTT​ (2 propagating polarisations). Tensors are gauge invariant at linear order and decouple from scalars/vectors at linear order.

What changes when the metric is quantised

  • A test scalar on fixed gˉ​ has no back-reaction: δgμν​=0. Quantising hμν​ promotes the geometry itself to operators; the scalar's Tμν​ sources h (□h∼8πGT), giving O(G) corrections that are cubic/quartic gravitational and matter-gravity vertices.
  • Gauge: quantisation requires gauge fixing (e.g., de Donder) with Faddeev–Popov ghosts, or reduction to gauge-invariant variables; the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints remove the unphysical components. In dS there is no spatial infinity/time-translation Killing energy: "energy" and the S-matrix have no standard meaning (Abbott–Deser 1982 define charges perturbatively).
  • Renormalisation: GR is perturbatively non-renormalisable ('t Hooft–Veltman 1974; Goroff–Sagnotti 1986 two-loop divergence). It is a valid effective field theory below Mp​ (Donoghue 1994) with controlled corrections ∼H2/Mp2​, but with dS-specific IR issues (secular growth of graviton loops, Tsamis–Woodard line of work; contested).

Not claimed

Quantising ϕ does not quantise gravity. We solve free tensor modes on a fixed background only; no graviton loops, no non-linear constraint solution, no nonperturbative de Sitter quantum gravity. The boundary of the fixed-background calculation is exactly O(H2/Mp2​) corrections, back-reaction and global issues (finiteness of the dS entropy, observables in a closed universe).
