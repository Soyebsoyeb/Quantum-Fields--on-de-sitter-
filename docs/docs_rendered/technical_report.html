Quantum Fields on de Sitter Spacetime — Technical Report

Scalar fields on a fixed four-dimensional de Sitter background: modes, vacuum structure, correlation functions, first-order in-in perturbation theory, tensor modes. Every statement is tagged: [A] analytic result, [S] symbolic verification, [N] numerical experiment, [P] perturbative approximation, [C] conceptual discussion, [U] unimplemented direction. Longer derivations are in docs/; each section names its file.

1. Scope and motivation

We study a quantum field on a prescribed de Sitter spacetime. This is quantum field theory in curved spacetime, not quantum gravity: the metric is a fixed classical background and no back-reaction is included (see §15, §18). De Sitter is the simplest maximally symmetric cosmological background and the leading-order geometry of inflation; it also exposes the basic conceptual issues of QFT in curved spacetime: the meaning of "vacuum", infrared sensitivity of light fields, and the breakdown of fixed-order perturbation theory by secular growth.

Implemented and validated: free scalar (all ν), Wightman function and its short-distance structure, mode-space Green functions, first-order tree-level contact correlator, tensor modes. Not implemented: loops, second order, renormalised ⟨Tμν​⟩, graviton interactions (§19).

3. de Sitter geometry (docs/de_sitter_geometry.md) [A][S]

Hyperboloid −X02​+∑Xi2​=H−2; flat chart ds2=−dt2+e2Htdx2 covers half of it and is past-incomplete; static and global charts are different domains and are not interchangeable. SymPy computes R=12H2 in the cosmic, conformal and static-patch charts and Rμν​=3H2gμν​ [S].

4. The scalar-field action (docs/scalar_action.md) [A][S]

S=−21​∫−g​[(∇ϕ)2+(m2+ξR)ϕ2]. Variation with δϕ∣∂​=0 gives (□−m2−ξR)ϕ=0; the Euler–Lagrange operator of a generic FRW metric equals −g​(□−m2−ξR)ϕ symbolically.

8. Vacuum structure (docs/vacuum_states.md) [A][C]

Bunch–Davies: the Fock vacuum of the mode that is positive frequency at the chart's early boundary; equals the Euclidean vacuum and is SO(1,4)-invariant where the two-point function exists; one member of the α-vacuum family; for ν≥3/2 no invariant Hilbert-space vacuum (Allen–Folacci). A Hankel-free construction (adiabatic vacuum of order 0,2,4) converges to the same mode as ∣η0​∣→∞ (Fig. 3c) [N].

9. Wightman functions and propagators (docs/wightman_functions.md, docs/propagators.md) [A][N]

G+(η,r)=∫(2π)3d3k​ϕk​ϕk∗​eik⋅r, defined as a distribution by Abel regularisation (subtract 1/2k, exactly transformed). Closed form 16π2H2​Γ(23​+ν)Γ(23​−ν)2​F1​(23​+ν,23​−ν;2;21+Z​), Z=2ηη′η2+η′2−r2​, satisfying the invariant ODE. Mode-space G+,F,R,A with DGF=−iδ, DGR=δ; retarded function state-independent. GR vs an independent ODE solve: max diff 4.0e−12; derivative jump 1.0000000001.

13. Infrared and ultraviolet behaviour (docs/infrared_structure.md, docs/ultraviolet_structure.md) [A][N]

IR: ∣ϕk​∣2∝k−2ν; ∫k2∣ϕ∣2dk converges iff ν<23​; ν=23​ gives ⟨ϕ2⟩=4π2H2​N; m≪H saturates near 3H4/8π2m2 (matches the stochastic Fokker–Planck equilibrium as a benchmark). UV: G=A/u+Blnu+C+O(ulnu) with A=H2/16π2, B=(49​−ν2−2)A, C=B[ψ(23​+ν)+ψ(23​−ν)+2γE​−1] (DLMF 15.8.10; B cross-checked by Frobenius recursion; all three checked numerically). Leading singularity = flat space. Hadamard condition described [C]; sharp cutoffs shown to give Λ2 divergence and are not renormalisation.

14. Renormalisation issues (docs/renormalization.md) [C] + one explicit subtraction

One explicitly defined point-splitting subtraction (1/4π2σ2 and Blnμ2σ2): ⟨ϕ2⟩μ​=A/3+C+Bln(H2/4μ2). It is not presented as a complete covariant renormalised quantity: the μ-dependence and the choice of parametrix (Δ1/2 shifts the constant by H2/16π2) are finite ambiguities; no ⟨Tμν​⟩ and no loop renormalisation of λ is attempted.

15. Tensor perturbations (docs/tensor_perturbations.md) [S][A][C]

Linearised vacuum Einstein equations (SymPy) give hk′′​−η2​hk′​+k2hk​=0. With the standard quadratic action (quoted), each polarisation is a massless minimal scalar and Δt2​=2H2/π2Mp2​; numerical integration gives 0.2026426 vs 0.2026424 (H=Mp​=1, x=10−3). Quantising a test scalar does not quantise gravity; gauge fixing, constraints, non-renormalisability and the EFT interpretation are conceptual only.

16. Numerical methodology [N]

Mode ODE: real 4-vector state, DOP853, rtol 10−4–10−13, atol≈10−2rtol. Integration η0​=−80→η1​=−0.02, k=1. Hankel functions: scipy.special (real order) / mpmath (imaginary order). In-in integrals: contour rotation with scipy.quad (double) or mpmath.quad (40 digits). Distributional k-integrals: QUADPACK QAWF. Environment: Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.4.4, SciPy 1.17.1, SymPy 1.14.0, mpmath 1.3.0, Matplotlib 3.10.8. Each experiment writes a JSON record (parameters, diagnostics, data hash, environment) to results/.

18. Limitations (docs/limitations.md)

Fixed background only; flat chart only; first-order tree-level interaction only; scheme-dependent point splitting; IR cutoff dependence for ν≥3/2; double-precision cancellation at late times for ν=21​,23​ (mitigated by mpmath); tensor-action normalisation quoted; references not network-verified. Numerical agreement validates, but does not prove, the analytic statements.

19. Future research directions [U]

(i) Second-order and exchange diagrams, one-loop tadpole with covariant Hadamard renormalisation; (ii) α-vacua and their Hadamard violation; (iii) stochastic/Fokker–Planck resummation compared with fixed-order in-in; (iv) global-chart and static-patch (thermal) analysis; (v) ⟨Tμν​⟩ and back-reaction; (vi) scalar and vector metric perturbations with constraints; (vii) graviton interactions and effective-field-theory loops; (viii) Euclidean/sphere methods and dS/CFT-like boundary correlators.
