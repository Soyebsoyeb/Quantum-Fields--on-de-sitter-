Tree-level correlators: the contact trispectrum

Status: perturbative approximation (order λ, tree level, no loops), analytic + numerical.

Observable

The connected, momentum-stripped four-point function of the field at fixed time,

⟨ϕk1​​ϕk2​​ϕk3​​ϕk4​​⟩conn′​(η)=−2λH4η4Im[i=1∏4​vki​∗​(η)I(η)],I=∫−∞η​dη′i∏​vki​​(η′).

Derivation. HI​=4!λ​∫d3xχ4. Contract the four χ's of the vertex with the four external fields: 4! ways, cancelling the 1/4!; each contraction gives ⟨χk​(η′)χk​(η)⟩′=vk​(η′)vk∗​(η) (vertex to the left). Momentum conservation from ∫d3x. Convert χ=aϕ for the four external legs: a−4=H4η4. Contractions of the vertex with itself are tadpole/disconnected and are not part of the connected correlator.

Specification. Vertex −i4!λ​χ4 (contact); external modes vki​​; time range (−∞,η]; vacuum free BD, iϵ (Abel) via contour rotation η′=η−(1+i)s; Fourier normalisation (2π)3δ(3) stripped; order λ1; no regulator needed (the integral converges on the rotated contour). The numerical contour integral reproduces this to ∼10−16 at all tested η (Fig. 9a); the η4 scaling is a−4 — the interaction does not evolve in conformal variables.

Cross-checks of the machinery

  1. Contour rotation vs real-axis Abel damping. Iϵ​=∫eϵKη′∏v along the real axis tends linearly in ϵ to the rotated-contour value (Fig. 9b).
  2. Exact diagonalisation of the QM analogue (above).
  3. Double vs extended precision: the two backends agree to 10−8 at ν=1.

Massless minimal field: logarithm

For ν=3/2, expand ϕk​(η′)=2k3​H​(1+2k2η′2​−3ik3η′3​+…). The term ∝η′3 in a4∏ϕ gives ∫dη′/η′, so

⟨ϕ4⟩conn′​ η→0​ 24∏ki3​λH4∑ki3​​ln(−η)+const.
Numerically, the slope with respect to ln(−η) equals this coefficient to better than 10−6 (relative) at ∣η∣∼10−5/k (Fig. 9c). This is a secular term: ∝λN with N=ln(1/k∣η∣).

Perturbative validity (numerical experiment, Fig. 10)

Ratio R=⟨ϕ4⟩conn(1)​/Pk2​ (equal momenta, Pk​=∣ϕk​∣2) vs N: ν=1/2 constant (−1/8)λ (conformal invariance); ν=1 and 5/4 saturate; ν=3/2 grows linearly in N. Consequently for light fields the fixed-order result becomes unreliable once λN∼1, i.e. N∼1/λ e-folds; for the massive fields the saturation scale is set by 1/(3/2−ν)∼3H2/2m2. This is a limitation of the fixed-order expansion for these observables, not evidence that the theory is inconsistent: resummation (Starobinsky stochastic dynamics, Starobinsky–Yokoyama 1994; Hollands 2013; Marolf–Morrison 2011) controls the IR sector. Those methods are described but not derived here.
