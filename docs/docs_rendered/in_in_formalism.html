In-in (Schwinger–Keldysh) formalism

Status: derivation; implemented at first order for contact diagrams (in_in.py). Higher orders: unimplemented.

Why not in–out

An in–out amplitude ⟨out∣S∣in⟩ requires asymptotic regions with well-defined particle states and a vacuum-persistence structure. In an expanding patch there is no late-time asymptotic region with a free-particle interpretation (η→0− is a spacelike boundary), and what is measured are expectation values ⟨Ω∣Q(η)∣Ω⟩ at a finite time in a given initial state, not transition amplitudes. The in-in expectation value uses U†QU — both the forward and backward evolution — so the final state is never specified.

Formula

⟨Q(η)⟩=⟨Ω​Tˉei∫−∞η​HI​QI​(η)Te−i∫−∞η​HI​​Ω⟩.

Closed-time path: the contour runs −∞→η (time-ordered branch "+", vertices −iHI​) then back η→−∞ (anti-time-ordered "−", vertices +iHI​); the vertical segment closing the contour at −∞ is controlled by the iϵ (Abel) prescription η′→−∞(1∓iϵ) on the ± branches so that the interacting vacuum is projected out of the free one.

Assumptions. (i) ∣Ω⟩ is the free BD vacuum; (ii) coupling switched on adiabatically; (iii) HI​ has no derivative couplings (so HI​=−LI​); (iv) first order in λ — higher orders require time-ordered nested integrals and renormalisation.

First order

⟨Q⟩(1)=i∫−∞η​dη′⟨[HI​(η′),Q(η)]⟩=−2Im∫ηdη′⟨HI​(η′)Q(η)⟩.

The commutator ("retarded") form manifestly has support only on η′<η, and each term is finite when Im→ cancels the real divergent pieces; this is why the Im is computed before integrating in the extended-precision backend (cancellation ∼(k∣η∣)−7 at late times in double precision).

Equivalence of the two forms

schwinger_keldysh_branches returns the "+" branch contribution −i∫⟨QH⟩=−iX∗ and the "−" branch +i∫⟨HQ⟩=iX; their sum is −2ImX, identical to the commutator form (tested).

Independent validation

The 0+1-dimensional analogue H=p2/2+ω2q2/2+gq4/4! has the same formula, κ4(1)​=−g/32ω5, and the exact ground state obtained by diagonalising H in a truncated oscillator basis reproduces the slope to 1.6×10−6 relative (Fig. 11). Sign and normalisation of the in-in expression are therefore not merely derived but cross-checked against a non-perturbative computation.
